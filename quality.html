Quality & compliance

Almost every dispute in food export traces back to a specification nobody wrote down. This is how we stop that happening.

The principle

Agree it in writing, then prove it

A buyer and a seller can use the same word for two different things. "Good quality turmeric" means nothing at the point where a container is rejected. A curcumin percentage, a moisture ceiling and a packing spec mean something.

Our quality process is not a certificate on a wall. It is a sequence: write the specification down, buy from someone who can meet it, check the goods before they move, and hand over documents that let your side clear them.

How it runs

Five controls on every order

Specification

The parameters that decide acceptance — moisture, purity, size, colour, packing, shelf life — are written into the order before it is placed.

Vendor assessment

We buy from suppliers who genuinely handle that product at that grade, not from whoever quotes lowest. Capacity and past performance both count.

Sampling

Samples are drawn from the actual lot being offered, not a showroom bag, and sent for your approval where the grade matters.

Pre-shipment inspection

Goods are checked against the written specification before packing and stuffing. Third-party inspection can be arranged where you want it.

Testing

Laboratory analysis — aflatoxin, pesticide residue, heavy metals, microbiology — arranged where the destination market or your own customer requires it.

Documentation

Export paperwork prepared for the destination's requirements and shared for checking before the vessel sails.

What a specification should cover

For most food commodities, these are the parameters worth fixing in writing. Not all apply to every product — but the ones that do should be numbers, not adjectives.

ParameterWhy it matters
Moisture contentDrives shelf life, mould risk and weight. The single most common cause of claims.
Purity / admixtureHow much of the cargo is the product and how much is everything else.
Size or countGrade and price band — grain length, chickpea count, egg weight, chilli length.
ColourObjective where a scale exists (ASTA for chilli), agreed against a sample where it does not.
Broken / defective %Decides the commercial grade on rice, pulses and nuts.
Active contentCurcumin in turmeric, pungency in chilli, oil content in seeds.
Contaminant limitsAflatoxin, pesticide residue, heavy metals — set by your destination market.
Packing & net weightBag type, liner, net and gross weight, marks and labelling.
Shelf life at despatchHow much life the goods have left when they leave, not when they were made.

If you are not sure which parameters your market enforces, describe the end use and the destination. Advising on that is part of the service, not an extra.

Inspection and third parties

We inspect goods ourselves before despatch as a matter of course. Where you want independent verification — because your own customer requires it, or because the order is large enough to justify it — third-party inspection by an agency of your choosing can be arranged and built into the schedule.

Where an inspection finds goods outside specification, you will hear about it from us before the container is sealed. An inconvenient result reported early costs far less than a rejected consignment at the destination port.

Documentation

Which documents apply depends on the product and the destination. These are the ones that come up on a typical food or industrial shipment out of India:

  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading or airway bill
  • Certificate of origin
  • Phytosanitary certificate
  • Veterinary / health certificate
  • Fumigation certificate
  • Certificate of analysis
  • Insurance certificate
  • Inspection certificate

Import requirements differ by country and change over time. Tell us the destination early and we will confirm what your side needs before the goods move.
